Understanding Report Rating Codes

In compliance with Massachusetts home inspection reporting guidelines, systems and components are categorized using standardized ratings:

A Acceptable

Inspected and performing its intended design function without observed significant defects.

M Marginal / Wear

Operational but nearing the end of useful life, showing routine wear, or requiring routine homeowner maintenance.

D Defective

Component is damaged, inoperable, missing, or requires repair or evaluation by a licensed specialist.

! Immediate Action

Identified safety hazards, fire risks, or severe structural threats requiring immediate professional remediation.

NI Not Inspected

Component was present but not accessible due to personal storage, snow cover, safety risks, or lack of utilities.

Comprehensive Scope

What Is Evaluated in Every Report

Every Massachusetts home inspection covers all readily accessible structural, mechanical, and safety components of the property.

Roof & Drainage

Roof coverings, visible underlayment, valleys, flashing at chimneys & skylights, gutters, and downspout discharge distances from foundations.

Exterior & Grading

Siding, trim, eaves, soffits, fascia, decks, ledger attachments, handrails, steps, exterior doors, patio settlement, and perimeter slope drainage.

Basement & Structure

Foundation walls (poured concrete, fieldstone, block), sill plates, floor joists, structural support posts, efflorescence/moisture staining, and crawlspaces.

Electrical Systems

Service drops, meters, main breaker panels, subpanels, double-tapped breakers, bonding bars, branch wiring, GFCI/AFCI receptacles, and fixture operation.

Plumbing & Water

Main water shutoff valve, supply and distribution piping, drain/waste/vent lines, water heaters, TPR relief valves, faucets, functional flow, and drainage.

Heating & Cooling

Boilers, furnaces, heat pumps, central A/C units, operating controls/thermostats, distribution ductwork, radiators, flue vent pipes, and air filters.

Attic & Insulation

Attic rafters and roof sheathing, presence of water staining or moisture, insulation levels, continuous soffit and ridge ventilation, and exhaust ducts.

Interior & Bathrooms

Windows, window operators, representative interior doors, walls, ceilings, stairs, railings, countertops, cabinets, exhaust ventilation fans, and built-in appliances.

Why do I need a Home Inspection in Massachusetts?

The home inspection is a visual non invasive inspection that makes note of deficiencies that make a home unsafe. Safety is the number one priority with your home inspection.

The home inspection also gives home sellers a chance to make needed repairs to their home, with the contractors of their choosing, prior to listing their home on the market.

A home inspection provides home buyers with the information needed to budget for repairs and/or negotiate on a home sale price.

In all instances the home inspection and home inspection report is an objective 3rd party view of the home.

What does the Home Inspection cover?

Structural:
  • Roofing
  • Chimney/Fireplace
  • Exterior Siding
  • Gutters & Down Spouts
  • Grounds
  • Attic/Basement/Framing
  • Garage
  • Foundation
  • Patio/Deck/Porches
Mechanical:
  • Water Heaters
  • Electrical
  • Heating Systems
  • Cooling Systems
  • Plumbing Systems
  • Walls/Ceilings
  • Doors/Windows
  • Kitchens

How long does the Home Inspection take?

An average home inspection can last around 2-4 hours.

Timing varies depending on square footage, accessibility of the attic and crawlspaces, and the overall age and configuration of the mechanical systems.

How much does a Home Inspection cost?

The cost for a home inspection starts at $475.00 and goes up depending on the square footage, age, and bedrooms of the property.

Move in move out inspections start at $125.00.
